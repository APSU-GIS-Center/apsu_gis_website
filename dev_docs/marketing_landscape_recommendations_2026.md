# APSU GIS Center: 2026 Marketing Landscape & Funding Recommendations

## Scope
- Geography: Tennessee + Southern Kentucky
- Primary objective: Grant and contract wins
- Priority audiences: City/county leaders, emergency management/public safety, economic development organizations

## Research Signals (External)
1. **APSUGIS.org current positioning** highlights mission and project examples, but conversion messaging is broad and not consistently tied to funding outcomes.
2. **TNGIC ecosystem** emphasizes statewide GIS collaboration, annual/regional convenings, jobs, and partner ecosystem growth.
3. **TNGIS clearinghouse** reflects broad Tennessee demand across cadastral, elevation/LiDAR, imagery, infrastructure, addresses, social/economic, and weather datasets.
4. **KyGeoNet + KyGovMaps/KyFromAbove** shows Kentucky also has mature statewide geospatial infrastructure and open-data channels for cross-border projects.
5. **State/local GIS market direction** (Esri gov guidance) continues to prioritize emergency management, public works/infrastructure, land administration, and economic restoration.

## Opportunity Areas for APSU GIS Center

### 1) Grant-Ready Public Safety Programs
**Why now:** Regional agencies need fast, defensible geospatial outputs for preparedness and response funding.

**Offer package:**
- Incident pre-plan maps + critical asset inventory
- Rapid post-event imagery/drone assessment workflow
- Dashboard + reporting templates aligned to grant narratives

**Marketing angle:**
- "From hazard exposure to funding-ready action plan in 30 days."

### 2) Infrastructure Resilience & Asset Intelligence
**Why now:** TNGIS and KyGeoNet data categories indicate sustained demand for infrastructure and elevation/imagery use cases.

**Offer package:**
- Utility/public works asset baselines
- Risk overlays (flood, elevation, access)
- Prioritized capital improvement mapbooks/dashboards

**Marketing angle:**
- "Turn existing GIS layers into capital-priority evidence for boards and funders."

### 3) Economic Development Competitiveness
**Why now:** Economic development teams need fast site and workforce intelligence to recruit investment and justify incentives.

**Offer package:**
- Site selection scorecards
- Workforce and commute shed analytics
- Executive-ready "community snapshot" dashboards

**Marketing angle:**
- "Improve recruitment speed and proposal confidence with location intelligence."

## Messaging Changes Needed (Site-Wide)
1. Lead with **decision-maker outcomes**, not capabilities alone.
2. Add **procurement-friendly language**: scope, deliverables, timeline, and implementation phases.
3. Use a recurring **proof pattern**: problem → geospatial intervention → measurable outcome.
4. Clarify regional footprint (Tennessee + Southern Kentucky) and ideal client profiles.
5. Promote "consultation" and "roadmap" CTAs over generic "contact us" where possible.

## Suggested 90-Day Marketing Actions

### Days 1–30
- Publish 3 audience pages or sections:
  - City/County Leadership
  - Emergency Management & Public Safety
  - Economic Development Organizations
- Add one downloadable **Capabilities + Deliverables one-pager** (PDF).

### Days 31–60
- Launch monthly thought-leadership posts:
  - Grant-ready data packages
  - NG911/data readiness
  - Site selection and workforce analytics
- Add lead form routing by project type and timeline.

### Days 61–90
- Run a webinar briefing for local agencies:
  - "Funding-Ready GIS for 2026"
- Build partner referral channel with regional planning/economic organizations.
- Add KPI dashboard for pipeline and conversion measurement.

## Recommended KPI Targets
- Consultation requests per month (by audience segment)
- Qualified opportunities per quarter
- Win rate for funded proposals/contracts
- Time-to-proposal package delivery
- Repeat client engagement rate

## Risks to Address
- Placeholder/template-era content can dilute trust for public-sector buyers.
- Generic testimonials without measurable outcomes reduce procurement confidence.
- Lack of explicit funding workflow language may underperform with decision-makers under budget pressure.
